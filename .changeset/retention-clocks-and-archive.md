Retention gains separate clocks, content-only pruning and sealed archives written and checked before anything is deleted. `retention: 30d` and the 30-day default run exactly as before.

- `retention:` may be written out as `{ content, metadata, failed }`, in `defaults:` or a channel. `content` empties a done message's bodies, error texts and `ctx.meta` once it is that old and keeps its row; `metadata` deletes the row (30 days when unset); `failed` deletes a failed message (kept until replayed or cleared when unset). A written-out block also deletes, on the metadata clock, a message whose content was dropped; the shorthand still leaves it. Any block counts as written out, so adding only `archive` or `audit` to `retention:` switches this on: keep the shorthand, or set `metadata:` as long as those rows should stay. `metadata` shorter than `content` is refused when the project loads. `pathways replay` refuses a message whose content went with `content-dropped`, as for `dropAfterDelivery`.
- `retention.archive: { dir }` in `pathways.yaml` writes every batch retention is about to change to a sealed archive first, every column of each message's row and of each of its deliveries, every generation: its `ctx.meta`, error texts and every body among them. Gzipped NDJSON with a header (format version, channel, key id, created, step) and a trailer (count and an HMAC-SHA-256 over the records), encrypted with the envelope stored messages use, one data key per file. Each is written to a `.partial` file, flushed, renamed, its folder flushed, read back and checked, and recorded as a new `archive` audit action, and only then are its messages changed, in batches of at most 500. A crash leaves a duplicate archive, never a loss. When an archive cannot be written, checked or audited, that channel is left alone for the run and the engine logs `retention-skipped` with the channel and code; `pathways retention` prints it and exits 1. `archive` with `encryption: none` is refused at load.
- `retention.audit` takes `never` alone, the default: the audit log is never pruned.
- `pathways retention --dry-run` prints each clock's counts.
- New in the API: the optional `MessageStore` methods `countExpiring`, `listExpiring`, `applyExpiry` and `archiveRows` (the `RetentionStore` type), with `RetentionQuery`, `RetentionStep`, `MessageRows`, `StoredMessageRow` and `StoredDeliveryRow`; the archive record types `ArchiveHeader`, `ArchivedMessage`, `ArchivedDelivery` and `ArchiveErrorCode`; `archive` in `AuditAction` and `AuditEventAction`. A custom store needs no change unless a project sets the content or failed clock or an archive: then it implements the four methods, and without them retention leaves its channels alone with `ERR_PATHWAYS_RETENTION_UNSUPPORTED`. Code that switches over `AuditAction` or `AuditEventAction` gains one case to handle.
